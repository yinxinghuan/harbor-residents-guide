A small community can still play

First test the fun of shoreline discovery, then test complementary livelihoods and shared town improvements. Solo play must remain viable. Cooperation adds choices, while clearly labelled NPC support provides a fallback.

This reading edition combines the core gameplay candidates and the phased multiplayer proposal. The three new candidates have not yet established a main loop through playtesting. Code reuse, capacity and delivery estimates still need verification.

Planning status: capture mechanics, exchanges, shared construction and avatar customization below are candidates or future proposals. This is not a list of released features or a release-date commitment.

Three candidates for the core loop

Shoreline discovery: learn to read clues

In 30 seconds, notice movement by a burrow, approach or pause, then try one capture action; failure is easy to retry. Within five minutes, choose between two places using clues, make a discovery and decide whether to observe, bring it back or explore elsewhere. Across days, sunshine, rain and new access change the opportunities and make learned patterns useful.

  • Initially carry at most two physical finds; observation notes take no space. Rare finds need readable, repeatable conditions.
  • A resident response should change the next outing, for example through a weather clue or access opportunity. Test fixed causes and effects first, without presenting scripted reactions as autonomous reasoning.
  • Risk: a fixed collection route sustained only by new species. The third encounter should still invite a new decision without adding species.

Complementary livelihoods: choose where a harvest goes

In 30 seconds, harvest and choose personal use, seed saving or exchange. Within five minutes, gather independently or trade toward an upgrade that saves labour or opens a new activity. Across days, develop changeable abilities and adjust plans after asynchronous exchanges. Avoid locked professions and mandatory daily deliveries.

  • Start with a small exchange board showing offered items, requested items, quantity, expiry and cancellation refunds. Initial ratios are fixed and transparent; player pricing comes later.
  • Minimum scope: two real people, two outputs, two upgrades and a few offers. Unanswered offers must not block progress; absence brings no debt, dead crops or relationship penalty.
  • Risk: trades driven only by subsidies, money grinding, one person doing everything, or a partner’s decisions having no effect on the next round.

Shared town improvements: continue what someone began

In 30 seconds, clear a short path and see the map acknowledge the contribution. Within five minutes, decide whether the same timber should protect a shared garden or open a walkway; one person can finish in steps. Across days, one person begins, another continues later, and both use the result before reconsidering priorities as weather and goals change.

  • Limit the test to one small community, two places, two weather conditions, two projects and one pool of materials. Do not gate public benefits by contribution size or rank players by labour.
  • Layout changes need explicit permission. Contributions cannot be casually destroyed; removal requires agreed confirmation, public-material refunds and recovery.
  • Residents can use the new walkway and respond to shared facts, while personal relationships and private experiences remain individual.
  • Risk: material errands, latecomers only claiming rewards, the same answer every round, or progress requiring everyone to attend.

From asynchronous cooperation to shared sessions

Stage 0 · Validate fun and scope

Run the small discovery test, then evaluate livelihood and construction choices with a few fixed participants. Read-only inventory identifies reusable, changed and new identity, save, resource and map systems. Paper bookkeeping tests rule clarity, not networking, physical interaction or retention.

Stage 1 · Close one asynchronous cooperation loop

Use one community, one complementary need and one facility. A collects materials and leaves a clear request; B later chooses an exchange or contribution; the facility gains one visible use; A returns and replans. Personal goals and NPC fallback remain available. Exclude open markets, free construction, cross-community transfers and real-time avatars.

Stage 2 · Add real time only when it adds value

Trial brief sessions for 2–4 people on top of asynchronous rules: one small map, few objects and fixed actions such as coordinated carrying or repairs. Solo alternatives remain. Results return to the same community progress, and interrupted actions must be resolved. If simultaneous presence adds nothing, keep cooperation asynchronous.

Stage 3 · Expand in response to evidence

Expand activities before player counts or an open world. Require evidence of simultaneous demand, governance, recovery and sustainable operations. Low activity should not destroy existing worlds. An open world is not scheduled.

Avatar customization: standardize before generation

Start with a limited set of tested parts

Use a shared body template with limited hair, clothes, skin tones and colours. Appearance must not change speed, collision or reach; names remain separate from stable identity. Fix canvas size, map pixel scale, foot anchors, directions, frame counts, timing, occlusion and tool grips before expanding assets. Exact specifications still need verification.

Check every part in idle, walking and work actions, especially hats, hair, sleeves and tool overlap. Version part identifiers and fall back to a default avatar when a part is unavailable.

Then review user-image generation

Prefer portraits or appearance references first. A full map avatar must pass every direction and action check. Explain purpose, processing, visibility, retention and deletion before upload; real-person portraits are not required. Publish to other players only after review; keep presets on failure and support reports, hiding, replacement and withdrawal.

Keep uploads closed until animation compatibility, review ownership and deletion are established. If frame-by-frame repair stays expensive, limit generation to portraits.

Rules for a shared world

Identity, progress and time
  • Identity, appearance, discoveries, abilities and inventory are personal; facilities and materials belong to the community. Relationships stay personal, public facts stay consistent, and positions and brief actions belong to a session.
  • Use invited fixed members and read-only guests, with visible join, exit and management-transfer rules. Departing members keep personal progress without taking built public facilities.
  • One authority determines community time and weather. Bound offline growth; absence never damages relationships or facilities. Solo play does not imply offline transactions. Shared submissions await connection, and device clocks do not decide rewards.
Items and exchanges
  • Item batches have unique identities and owners. Shared resources are claimed once, with clear limits and refresh rules; reconnecting or changing a device clock adds nothing. Prefer personal discovery opportunities over racing to spawns.
  • Escrow offered items, show expiry and refund on cancellation. Begin with fixed items and quantities, excluding auctions, loans and unrestricted currency transfers.
  • A trusted service verifies identity, permission, ownership, quantities and offer versions. Deductions and delivery commit atomically or through explicit compensation. Stable request IDs prevent replay, reject changed payloads and allow only one competing acceptance.
  • Success, pending, cancellation and failure are queryable. Track origins and transfers to prevent duplication, negative inventories and alternate-account arbitrage.
Construction, NPCs and live recovery
  • Preview uses, materials and affected areas before confirmation. Protect routes, spawn and exit points; keep the existing layout while disputes remain unresolved.
  • Record before/after state, costs and dependencies. Do not roll back an entire town over later dependent work; refund removals once to public storage. Provide reporting, blocking, membership removal and recovery.
  • NPCs share public route facts without revealing personal relationships or inventing player contributions.
  • Live rooms share map versions, object IDs, join snapshots and event sequences. Validate interaction distance and time-limited work locks server-side. Reconnect by checking committed outcomes first, then returning to a safe location. Keep confirmed progress when a host leaves.
Existing-save migration

Back up saves and rehearse in isolated copies, mapping characters, appearance, discoveries, items and abilities without silently dropping unknown fields. Never bind identity by nickname. Preserve old solo worlds and transfer verified items only by choice. Migration reruns must not duplicate rewards; after new trades, use consistent recovery or event compensation rather than restoring one old inventory.

What evidence supports further investment?

Discovery: the same objects under three conditions

Follow clues in sunshine, adapt after rain, then use an existing clue to reach a new place without adding species. Begin with movement and one nearby action, one crab, two places, three clues, two carried items and a brief record. The first new discovery should arrive within five minutes.

Precheck on a real phone. Small-sample gates: at least two of three participants start independently within 30 seconds, at least two later adapt to conditions and explain why, and at least two choose a further goal and try it for a minute in a neutral continuation window. Fix interaction failures before judging the theme. These gates are not statistical or retention findings.

Test discovery independently, then compare versions with and without a resident response that changes the next outing; match tool rewards. Record actions, prompts, comments and reasons for continuing. Scheduled return tests are not natural retention.

Cooperation: solo, asynchronous, then live with absence

Two real participants first complete independent goals, then A leaves a request and B chooses how to respond, followed by a short live session in which one leaves. Match solo rewards to avoid subsidizing cooperation. Livelihoods should change uses or tool plans because of a partner; construction should make both people recognize and use shared consequences; absence must not block the other player.

One participant can test only the solo foundation; NPCs cannot substitute for a second real person as evidence of multiplayer fun. After a two-person loop works, use 3–5 people to examine late entry, absence and domination.

Acceptance gates and reasons to stop expanding

  • Before asynchronous implementation: clear choices and shared consequences, viable solo progress, an inventory of current capabilities and an agreed scope.
  • Before expanding asynchronous play: reproducible checks for replay, competing offers, interrupted delivery, reconnection, two-device conflicts, member exit and save recovery. Duplication, loss, unauthorized changes and corruption block expansion.
  • Before real-time play: show its benefit over asynchronous cooperation and test positions, interactions, disconnections and room shutdown on target phones and weak networks. One smooth demonstration does not establish capacity.
  • Before an open world: evidence of sustained demand, governance, monitoring, recovery, operational coverage and costs.
  • If players only wait, grind, perform obligatory labour or repeat one optimal answer, fix rules or reduce scope. More maps, players and assets do not resolve that failure.
