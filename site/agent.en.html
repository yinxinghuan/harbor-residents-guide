How to read the evidence

  • Existing-version evidence: earlier release, play or test records support a feature, not fresh acceptance in this integrated version.
  • Candidate short test: only the named flow was checked, not the whole system or every device.
  • Design direction: an intended loop or rule, not an enabled event or integration.
  • Integration not verified: the full behavior in this Agent/multiplayer candidate is unconfirmed. It does not mean the feature is absent, incompatible or deleted.

One life world, new participants

Human players choose what they want to do. Agents choose using their goals, permitted information and available resources. World rules decide whether an action is valid and record its outcome.

Existing exploration and life systems supply places, resources, people and continuing goals. Agents add residents’ needs and actions; multiplayer puts participants into a shared world state.

The new capability needs three checks: do Agents act, can a player affect a later choice, and can others see a result that survives re-entry? Existing enabled life activities should remain understandable and playable in their own right.

Harbor’s complete game systems

These 12 existing systems remain part of Harbor. Open each for its play loop, intended Agent/multiplayer relationship and evidence boundary. An unverified integration does not mean an old feature has disappeared.

2.1 Maps, exploration and routes

What players do: Explore linked streets, the harbor, coast and courtyard; find residents and activity locations. Bridge repairs, routes and the market have source or release evidence.

Agent and multiplayer goal: The goal is consistent locations and access conditions for people and Agents. Shared places do not imply real-time visible movement together.

Evidence and limits: Linked streets have 19 October records; map acceptance closed on 24 October and is not reopened here. The 31 October record supports bridges, routes and the market. This candidate’s reuse and shared route state still need confirmation. Unlimited automatic expansion is not established.

2.2 Fishing and catches

What players do: Existing flows include fishing-point interaction, obtaining gear, casting and reeling, confirming a catch, inventory, bait purchase and a fish codex; earlier tests also involved sales. Use the actual interface, not an assumption that every water surface is fishable.

Agent and multiplayer goal: A catch could become material for a resident order, trade or commission. Ownership, recipient, quantity and settlement must be explicit and happen once. Agents changing plans after receiving a fish remains an integration goal.

Evidence and limits: A 22 October formal-version test covered a catch, persistence before claiming, inventory after confirmation, bait purchase and reloads. The old 1/12 codex display is not this version’s species limit. A 27 October fishing/gear-interface replacement exists; mobile feel was not fully verified. None of this proves shared Agent trading is connected.

2.3 Planting and plants

What players do: Basil and mint, obtaining and buying seeds, planting, watering and harvesting are established content or recorded flows. Caring for plants gives a continuing daily goal.

Agent and multiplayer goal: Resident needs could lead to purchasing, care or delivery cooperation. Private plots, shared plants, harvest ownership and permission to tend or collect need explicit rules. Competitive harvesting is not established.

Evidence and limits: Basil and mint have 19 October records. A 6 November candidate short test covered seed collection, planting, watering, harvesting and refresh persistence. It does not validate the full natural cycle, ecology or Agent-economy integration.

2.4 Time, day and night, and rest

What players do: Natural time and day/night are part of the existing world. Earlier records also show a nap changing time. Exploration, care, conversation and rest can form a daily rhythm.

Agent and multiplayer goal: The 2 November rule direction is a shared world clock: personal rest must not advance everyone else’s public clock. Agent decision periods and world time need to align; personal recovery and public time are separate.

Evidence and limits: Natural time has 19 October records, and a 30 October nap record shows a time change. This candidate’s common clock, rate and offline behavior remain unverified. One person sleeping must not be presented as global fast-forward, nor as established unattended 24-hour operation.

2.5 Weather and environment

What players do: Clear and rainy weather shape the feel of the town and coast. A candidate test observed overcast weather becoming light rain and persisting after refresh.

Agent and multiplayer goal: The goal is for a shared world to expose consistent weather facts. Residents adjusting plans, players rescheduling or passing on news still need concrete rules and actions.

Evidence and limits: Clear/rainy weather has 19 October records; the transition and reload short test is from 6 November. The complete natural cycle, shared weather and ecological effects are not fully confirmed. Typhoon scope, crop damage and yield probabilities are not established for this release.

2.6 Animal observation and ecology

What players do: Animals add observation and collection. A candidate registered a sunning cat and retained the entry after refresh; earlier records include dog and crab movement. Use only the offered observation and recording actions.

Agent and multiplayer goal: Observations could become a commission, a message or local knowledge. Whether they are personal collections, shared records or exchangeable information needs an explicit rule.

Evidence and limits: Dog/crab movement has 24 October records; the cat registration short test is from 6 November. Animation and records do not establish complete autonomous animal AI. Dynamic animal AI remains incomplete, and catching or farming every animal is not assumed.

2.7 Inventory, items and codices

What players do: Inventory stores owned catches, bait and seeds. Codices and observation records keep discoveries beyond a visit. Fish-codex and retained observation evidence exists.

Agent and multiplayer goal: Personal items must stay isolated. Transfers need a holder, quantity and recipient. Relationship entries, observations and tradable items are different: seeing a record does not give someone an item to gift.

Evidence and limits: Some inventory changes and persistence are evidenced, not every image in the asset library being a pickup. This candidate’s shared-trade consistency, cross-device behavior and persistence coverage still need confirmation.

2.8 Money, shops and resources

What players do: Seed and bait purchases and fish sales have existing-version or test records. Results can fund the next activity; money, items and labor have costs.

Agent and multiplayer goal: Agent buying and selling, making or accepting offers and trading with humans are goals. Shortages, an unsuitable price or another source should affect choices. Identify payer and recipient, and settle once.

Evidence and limits: Earlier isolated sandbox gifts, trades and settlements are separate evidence from old shop tests. Together they do not prove this shared economy is integrated. Available actions, autonomous trade and the shared-database loop remain to confirm; dynamic market pricing is not established live.

2.9 Resident dialogue and relationships

What players do: Conversation, shared experiences and character events build relationships and feedback. Mara, Avery, Samira, Owen and Dani have historic five-stage content.

Agent and multiplayer goal: Authored relationships can coexist with Agent goals, events and memory. Shared experience and commitment records should prevent contradictory outcomes. Public events, personal progress and private information need separate boundaries.

Evidence and limits: The first four residents have 19 October records; inclusion of Dani was confirmed on 23 October. Stage names are not invented here, and not every resident or autonomous interaction is complete. Links to Agent memory, privacy and human influence still need verification.

2.10 Quests, stories and world changes

What players do: Existing tasks give reasons to act: route guidance, bridge repairs that affect passage, opening routes and the market, character events, experiences and commitments. Some actions affect scene state.

Agent and multiplayer goal: Keep authored goals while Agent events add variation and opportunities. A resident need, a player decision, cooperation and an outcome affecting later action could form a dynamic task.

Evidence and limits: 31 October source/release evidence supports bridges, routes, the market, relationship experiences and commitments, plus harvests. Bounded side stories do not imply unlimited quests or arbitrary maps/buildings. Specific cooperative chains still need actual actions and acceptance.

2.11 Energy, rest, daily rhythm and training

What players do: Existing money, energy and inventory rules give choices costs; rest and naps let players recover. Training existed as a separate module and once affected fishing admission.

Agent and multiplayer goal: Reuse resource constraints where appropriate, but personal recovery is not a public-world time operation. State which modules are enabled and whether training still gates any activity.

Evidence and limits: Older rest and nap behavior has records; training is not this release’s focus. A global combat redesign is not established, and old training conditions are not automatically this version’s fishing requirements.

2.12 Identity, saves and returning

What players do: Personal identity, inventory and relationships have persistence evidence, as do particular catches, bait, plants and observations after re-entry or refresh.

Agent and multiplayer goal: Save personal state and shared facts separately, and retain checkable Agent events. Returning participants should keep the right identity and ownership, without duplicate actions or conflicting public outcomes.

Evidence and limits: A single-device or single-flow result does not validate real identity isolation, cross-device consistency, new shared storage or lossless old-save migration. Login, test accounts, retention and reset rules need explicit confirmation.

What Agents add

Harbor is a pixel life RPG with maps, residents, items and activities. The Agent goal is for residents to have things to do beyond waiting for fixed dialogue, influenced by money, materials, time, messages and other people.

Design example: one resident lacks materials for a job. Another has them but refuses the price. You could offer materials, pass on a message, join a commission or stay out of it.

This explains a design, not an event already open in this version.

The interest is understanding goals, information and constraints, then seeing how help or refusal changes a plan. Fishing, planting, exploration, relationships and quests supply the world in which that can happen.

Start with your own daily goal

  1. Try an enabled life activity.Explore a street, fish, care for a plant or meet someone. Understand its entry, resources and result before studying Agent mechanisms.
  2. Observe before intervening.Follow one resident or event. Use only interactions actually offered by the interface.
  3. Require real outcomes.Gifts change ownership, deliveries settle and promises name people and terms. Whether an old activity’s output can join these actions depends on enabled integrations.
  4. Watch later behavior.Does a new resource or message matter? You may also choose not to intervene.
  5. Compare two accounts.Public events should agree while inventory, identity and private information stay separate. Not every collection or relationship is shared.
  6. Return and check again.Do not repeatedly submit an uncertain action. Real-time co-presence, invitation rooms and live chat are not assumed available.

Observe, decide, execute, record

Observe: read permitted state, resources, events and memories, not everyone’s secrets.

Decide: a model selects legal action combinations. The rule layer is not another AI model; legal choices must also be tested for variety.

Execute: saying “done” is not enough. Check identity, ownership, resources, state and duplicates. Model text cannot bypass fishing, planting, shop or quest rules.

Record: outcomes become events and state for later decisions. Earlier isolated real-model actions, gifts, trades, cooperation and reloads do not validate this shared world.

Design baseline: 6→12 residents and four periods per game day

The original v0.4 proposal starts with six residents, then twelve, with four decision periods per game day. Each Agent has at most one decision, one main action and one limited communication per period; people may intervene between turns.

Not active settings: the actual population, frequency and limits still need the release checklist.

Shared facts, personal state and private knowledge

Separate common world facts such as open routes or completed public events; personal state such as inventory and relationship progress; and messages or memories known only to permitted recipients. Sharing a world does not expose everything.

How the systems could connect

All three are design examples, not enabled events or proof that a whole chain works. Individual evidenced steps remain separate from integration goals.

A · A fish that changes a resident’s plan

  1. Earlier evidence: catch and confirm a fish, retain it in inventory after reload.
  2. Integration goal: match an actual owned catch to an Agent’s need and permitted trade.
  3. Integration goal: an offer is accepted or refused according to resources and goals.
  4. Integration goal: transfer fish and money once with correct ownership. Earlier sandbox trades do not establish this fish-trade chain.
  5. Integration goal: the recipient changes a later action, visibly, because a resource really arrived.

Fishing can remain an independent activity even before this purchasing link exists.

B · From a seed purchase to a public route

  1. Earlier evidence: buy seeds and see inventory change.
  2. Earlier/candidate evidence: planting, care, harvest and refresh checks; full natural growth has separate limits.
  3. Integration goal: use a harvest in a commission or cooperative action with clear ownership and rewards.
  4. Mixed evidence and goal: old bridge/route changes do not prove a harvest-to-commission-to-construction chain.
  5. Integration goal: everyone and the residents use the same newly open route.

This does not make every crop a bridge material or allow arbitrary text to change a map.

C · Weather, relationships and an appointment

Rain could change a resident’s plans. You might reschedule, relay a message or try another activity, with later responses grounded in shared experience.

Earlier evidence: weather and relationships have separate records, and a candidate weather transition persisted. Integration goal: weather affecting schedules, messages changing decisions and correct privacy. No specific rainy-day event is established enabled.

A first visit and focused test route

Try an enabled life activity, then one Agent interaction. This is not a demand to retest every old feature or reopen closed specialist acceptance. Focus on actual integration changes and known risks.

  1. Confirm the entry and scope.Record version, device and time. Check enabled scenes, systems and Agents; walk a route and find an activity and return path.
  2. Try a life activity.Choose fishing, planting or another enabled flow. Check catch confirmation/inventory or seeds, care and harvest feedback. Do not search for an unenabled link just to complete a design example.
  3. Look at lasting records.Check inventory or a codex, then a resident conversation or quest. Distinguish tradable items, observations and personal relationships.
  4. Follow an Agent and interact once.Use an offered gift, trade or commission and compare resources before/after. Record what you see rather than assume background behavior.
  5. Observe the next response.Separate waiting for a decision period, an uncommitted action, missing feedback and unchanged behavior.
  6. Compare two accounts.Check enabled public events and private boundaries, without assuming shared collections or real-time co-presence.
  7. Return.Check identity, resources, records and outcomes. Avoid duplicate submissions and apply the stated test-data reset rules.

What to report

  • Understandable life activities: discoverable maps and activity entries, clear fishing/planting flows, visible gains, costs and records.
  • Credible Agents: purpose, responses to shortage/refusal/news, no pointless repetition or claims of outcomes that never occurred.
  • Player influence: clear opportunities, meaningful choices and visible consequences; which life results can actually be used?
  • Consistency: matching public results, no leaks, duplicate charges/rewards or lost records, and no confusion between personal and public state.
  • Clarity: distinguish waiting from being stuck; check mobile reading, scrolling, taps and relationship feedback.

Feedback template

  • Version and device.
  • Time.
  • Scene or system.
  • What you did.
  • What you expected.
  • What happened.
  • Whether it persists after refresh.
  • Screenshot or short video.

Do not send passwords, login tokens or credentials. Check the enabled scope before treating a design example as a defect.

The release checklist still needs

  • Entry, version, Agent roster/count, cadence and available actions.
  • Enabled maps, fishing, planting, time/weather, animals, inventory/codices, shops, relationships, quests, rest and training.
  • Which life systems remain independent, which connect to Agents, and the exact links.
  • Participant interactions and boundaries between public facts, personal state and private messages.
  • Login and test-account rules, a shared clock, personal rest and offline behavior.
  • Retention, resets or migration, known issues, disabled features and feedback destination.

The coordinator should supply this from actual release receipts, not leave testers guessing. Keep existing content, limited candidate tests, future design and integration results separate. Read the guide now; enabled scope must be updated with release.
